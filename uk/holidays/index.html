💡 Fixed vs. Islamic Holidays — and How to Plan Around Them

Fixed-date holidays (like New Year's Day or a national day) fall on the same Gregorian date every year, while Islamic (Hijri) holidays such as Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha follow a lunar calendar roughly 11 days shorter than the solar year, so they shift earlier each Gregorian year. The dates listed here are estimates based on astronomical calculation and aren't final until confirmed by an official moon-sighting announcement.

For planning: government offices and banks typically close on public holidays, so book appointments and travel in advance, especially around multi-day Eid periods. Some private businesses adjust their hours during these periods too — worth confirming opening times before planning anything time-sensitive.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions
Are the Islamic holiday dates confirmed?

No, Islamic-calendar dates (Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, etc.) are estimates based on expected moon sighting and are officially confirmed only a day or two before the event. Fixed Gregorian-calendar dates are confirmed.
